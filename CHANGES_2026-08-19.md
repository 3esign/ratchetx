# RATCHET — HARDENING PASS h1-2026-08-19

**What this is:** every fix from `RATCHET_DEEP_DIVE_2026-08-19.md`, plus the two things the
operator asked for on top: **rewards distributed on faster, dual clocks** (daily + weekly pots)
and **the Warden's public record accruing now** instead of waiting for a wave.
All 34 smoke checks pass (`test_harness.mjs`, stubbed oracle, no network).

The headline economics are **unchanged and still frozen: 70% burn · 30% pots · 0% creator.**
What changed inside the pot share is announced here, applies only to stakes from this deploy
forward, and is printed on the site itself. Nothing is retroactive.

---

## 1. NEW — rewards on two clocks (the "dynamic periods" ask)

The 30% pot share now splits half-and-half into two pots with two payout clocks:

- **DAILY POT** — top 3 of the day take **50 / 30 / 20%**, paid automatically at **00:00 UTC
  every day**. At today's player counts nobody should wait six days to feel a payout.
- **WEEKLY SEASON POT** — top 5 take **40 / 25 / 15 / 12 / 8%**, paid Sunday 00:00 UTC,
  exactly as before. The season stays the prestige ladder; the daily pot is the heartbeat.

Both use the same lazy, lock-guarded rollover (no cron, no button); unclaimed shares roll into
the next pot of the same cadence. The current live pot (accrued under the weekly-only rule)
stays entirely in the weekly pot — no retroactive reshuffling.

**Why not fully "dynamic" period lengths?** Because a period length that adapts to activity is
a knob wearing a formula's clothes — the exact thing the proof page promises doesn't exist.
Fixed daily + weekly boundaries derived from wall-clock give the fast-reward feel with zero
discretion. If a truly dynamic formula is ever wanted, it must be published and frozen first
(same rule as everything else here).

## 2. NEW — the Warden now keeps a public record

Every hourly Warden line is **sealed once** (first request of the hour wins a SET-NX; every
later request just reads it) and **settled on the same oracle** at its window's end. The page
shows lifetime record: settled calls, hit rate, Brier score — wins and losses alike.

This was the marketable core of "Wave 3" and it never needed the LLM — the machinery is what
matters, and it now runs. The LLM brain remains a drop-in replacement for the *reasoning text*
whenever a model host is chosen; nothing about sealing/settlement will change.

## 3. FIXED — honesty gaps from the deep dive

- **Burn attribution.** The front page no longer displays the graduation burn as if the game
  caused it. Player-verified burns (replay-gated, credited) are the headline; what pump.fun
  destroyed at graduation is stated separately as exactly that. New proof line: *"Burns
  attributed by cause, not bundled."* The incinerator line now correctly says manual reloads
  land there while one-click reloads burn straight from the payer's account.
- **RCX-priced targets removed.** At ~$10k market cap a player could settle their own sealed
  bet with a $50 trade — sealing hides your side from others, not from yourself. Targets now
  settle only on external majors via Pyth (SOL, BTC, ETH, BONK, WIF, JUP — 8 targets including
  two threshold shots). A proof line states this as policy. Any open RCX shots from the
  previous deploy auto-VOID with a full refund 24h after their expiry (see below).
- **Demo play is now truly unranked, server-side.** `demo-*` identities never reach any
  ladder, any pot payout, or the public kill feed. Before, "UNRANKED" was only a label — a
  script could farm the paying ladder with free throwaway identities.

## 4. FIXED — money-path bugs

- **Atomic replay gates.** Burn-reload and anchor crediting now use SET-NX: exactly one
  concurrent submission of a signature can credit. Before, two parallel submissions could
  both pass the check and double-credit.
- **Refunds return to their source.** A stake paid from credits refunds to credits. Before,
  an offline-feed refund converted burn-backed credits into free paper. Feed availability is
  also now checked *before* any money moves.
- **VOID accounting.** A voided (refunded) shot reverses its burn/pot contributions instead
  of feeding the counters with money nobody spent. The floor is monotone by construction
  (high-water mark) and voids no longer dilute the accuracy stat.
- **Pot payouts can't vanish.** If a payout loop fails mid-way, the one-shot lock is released
  and the next request retries. Before, a crash after the lock meant winners were silently
  never paid.
- **Strict KV reads on money paths.** A flaky Redis read now surfaces as an error instead of
  masquerading as "no data" — which could hand a player a fresh record that later overwrote
  their real one (credits included).
- **Stale-feed auto-void.** A shot whose feed disappears for good refunds itself 24h after
  expiry instead of hanging open forever.

## 5. FIXED — abuse resistance

- **Per-IP rate limiting** (80 GET / 20 POST per minute, in-memory per instance) — prices out
  naive loops and protects the KV quota.
- **`state?wallet=<anything>` no longer mints KV records** — player records persist only if
  they already existed or actually changed. Wallet strings are shape-validated.
- **Anchor XP pays once per wallet per 24h.** Anchoring stays open to everyone always; the
  cooldown just stops memo-spam (~half a cent per memo) being the cheapest XP in the game.
- **Input length caps** before base58/signature decoding.

## 6. CLIENT

Daily ladder + daily pot panel with yesterday's payouts; Warden record line; honest burn
chips (PLAYERS BURNED vs LAUNCHPAD/OTHER); XP progress bar measures within the current rank
band; expired sessions prompt a clean reconnect; title/naming pass ("The Machine That Cannot
Lie | $RCX"); copy updated everywhere the rules changed (fire button, how-to, proof lines).

## 7. ON THE WAVES — why some things wait and most don't

- **Waves 1–2: done and live** (game, token, real burns).
- **Wave 3 (Warden LLM): not blocked by engineering.** The scoring/sealing machinery now runs
  and accrues the record with the v0 heuristic. The only open decision is where the model runs
  (local Ollama vs paid API) and its cost. Ship it whenever you pick; nothing else changes.
- **Wave 4 (the redeemable vault) is the ONLY thing that should wait, and only for one
  reason:** it is custody, it must be immutable, and an immutable bug is forever. It waits for
  an audit, not for a calendar. Everything else in the old wave plan that could ship early
  has now shipped.

  carried inside the reloader's own signed transaction. The reloader still gets 1 credit per

  one grace period so a reload built just before midnight still verifies.
- Client: the one-click reload now builds burn + up to 3 TransferChecked legs

## THE HOLDER RULE — anti-dump without custody or vesting

- There is no deposit instruction, no pool, no key. The worst possible failure is paying zero

## UI pass (verified by rendered screenshots, desktop + mobile)

# h4-2026-08-19 — THE BLACK BOX: the machine can no longer be killed

The token was already unkillable (authorities revoked, liquidity protocol-held) and the code
was already unkillable (public repo). The state was the one mortal part — credits, ladders and
the log lived in one database behind one operator. h4 removes that mortality.

- **Full log retention.** Every hash-chained event is now stored permanently in 500-entry
  chunks (`g:log:c:N`), not just heads + a 60-entry window. The entire history to date was
  backfilled losslessly (it still fit inside the recent window — 41 entries at deploy time).
- **`GET /api/snapshot`** — the whole machine, downloadable by anyone: every player, board,
  replay-gated signature, the podium, the Warden's record, and the complete log, with a sha256
  over the canonical state. Cached 5 minutes. Read-only.
- **`restore.mjs`** — zero-dep verifier + loader. `--check` replays the full chain from
  sha256("ratchet-genesis") to the head and refuses tampered history (the harness plants a
  forged entry and the verifier names its exact index). Without `--check`, it loads a snapshot
  into a fresh Upstash KV; it refuses to overwrite a live instance unless forced.
- **`RESURRECTION.md`** — the stranger's guide: verify, restore, redeploy, ~15 minutes,
  no permission needed. Continuity is provable against the on-chain anchor memos.
- New proof line: **"The machine can be resurrected by anyone"** — live check that full
  retention is on, linking the snapshot.
- Honest limits, stated where they live: snapshots protect up to their moment (take them
  often); rule-immutability still requires the audited Wave-4 program; someone still has to
  host it — resurrection guarantees anyone MAY, forever, with proof.

The machine can be paused. It can no longer be killed.

## Also in h4 — THE BOARD, SHOT HISTORY, and the flicker fix

**THE BOARD.** Targets are now GENERATED, not hardcoded: a fresh mix every hour, deterministic
from the clock (seeded PRNG — every player and server instance derives the same board with no
coordination). Three evergreen anchors (SOL 5m, BTC 1h, ETH 24h) keep the rhythm; five rotating
slots keep it alive: a fast meme-feed directional, THE PUMP and THE DUMP (thresholds sized to
each feed's typical volatility), THE RACE (head-to-head relative performance of two feeds), and
THE BOX (does the hour end outside the band). New settle kinds `thrDown`, `range`, `race` — all
on external Pyth majors, all settle at expiry on the exit price, and labels now say "after",
never "within", because that is what the mechanics do. A sealed shot carries its own settlement
spec, so board rotation never touches an open bet; the previous hour's board stays valid as a
grace window for clicks that land across the flip. The UI shows a live "NEW MIX IN M:SS" clock.

**SHOT HISTORY.** Every settled shot is now recorded per player (`hist:<wallet>`, capped 200):
result, XP, stake, entry and exit price. The RANKS page grew a SHOT HISTORY panel (last 60,
color-coded, with lifetime accuracy in the header) — so hits and misses compound visibly instead
of falling off the end of a 3-row list. Included in the Black Box snapshot and restore.

**The flicker fix.** The client repainted every panel every second to tick countdowns, which
re-triggered entry animations — the flicker players reported. Now: `setHTML()` only touches the
DOM when content actually changed, and a surgical 1-second ticker updates countdown text nodes
directly. Chambers, kill feed, ladders and proof lists no longer flicker; animations play once.

# h6-2026-08-19 — SEALED MEANS SEALED: commit-reveal in the log

While mapping the on-chain path we audited our own core promise and found it leaking:
seal events in the hash-chained log recorded the player's SIDE in plaintext, and both the
proof page's recent-log view and `state?wallet=` served open shots with sides visible. An
open shot's side was technically readable before settlement — the exact thing "sealed"
promises cannot happen. Found by us, fixed by us, disclosed here.

- **Seals are now commitments.** A seal log entry carries `sha256("SIDE|salt")` — no side.
  The settle entry reveals `side` + `salt`, so anyone can verify every seal after the fact
  and nobody can read one before. (This is also Stage 2 of the on-chain path: the same
  commitment can be anchored on-chain per shot.)
- **Spectator views are blind.** `state?wallet=` and the Black Box snapshot serve open
  shots WITHOUT side/salt (commit included). Your own side is returned once, in your fire
  response, and your browser remembers it locally; on another device it shows 🔒 until
  settlement. Duel labels no longer reveal the stance either.
- **Resurrection rule:** snapshots never export sealed sides, so `restore.mjs` VOID-REFUNDS
  any still-open shots when bringing the machine back — stated in RESURRECTION.md.
- **Honest disclosure:** log entries written before h6 contain sides in plaintext. For
  already-settled shots that is public information anyway; shots still open at the h6
  deploy remain technically exposed until they settle (worst case: one 24h ETH window).
  Nothing can rewrite the hash chain, and we would not want a chain that could be rewritten.

# h7-2026-08-19 — STAGE 1a: daily balance roots, notarized by the chain

First concrete step of the on-chain migration path. At every daily rollover, the machine now
folds a BALANCE ROOT into the hash-chained log: sha256 over every player's (wallet, credits,
XP, burned), sorted, plus the machine stats. Because players anchor log heads into Solana memos,

existed long before the migration was announced — airdrop fairness, solved years early. The
latest root shows on the proof page's Black Box line. (~30 lines; scan cost is once per day.)

# h8-2026-08-20 — STAGE 3 ON-CHAIN, and every payout becomes a link

## The settlement program is live on devnet — and it ran a full lifecycle in public

`ratchet_seal` is deployed to Solana devnet at
**`4WQ4XTzC29M6YoxgNi9WHhYJWEtYyj6YNFtSB9yCM6E2`** and has executed seal → settle → reveal
end to end with no server in the loop. Receipts, with links, are in `ONCHAIN.md`.

What that proves, concretely:

- **seal** stores only `sha256("SIDE|salt")` and an entry price the program reads itself from a
  Pyth `PriceUpdateV2` account. The account is validated by hand — owner must be the Pyth
  receiver or push oracle, discriminator must match, verification level must be `Full`. No
  trusted deserialiser, no oracle wrapper we could quietly swap.
- **settle** is a permissionless crank. Anyone may call it after expiry, and only with a price
  whose `publish_time` lands inside `[expiry, expiry+60]`. During the live test the program
  refused to settle for a full minute because devnet's feed was publishing five seconds behind
  the chain clock — the staleness rule doing exactly its job, unattended.
- **reveal** recomputes the commitment, checks it, scores the outcome on-chain and bumps a
  `PlayerRecord` PDA. Its first-ever verdict was to mark the program author's own shot a MISS.

Honest scope: this is **devnet R&D**, not the live money path. The game you play today still
settles on our server — which is precisely why the proof page exists and why every claim on it
can turn red.

## Every money event in the kill feed is now a transaction you can open

The kill feed said `BURNED 5,000 RCX · +1,500 RCX to the podium` and asked you to take its word
for it. The signature was already recorded in the hash-chained log; it simply was not being
handed to the feed. Now it is: reload lines and log-anchor lines carry their signature and
render a `tx ↗` link to Solscan. A payout claim you cannot open is a claim you should not trust,
including ours.

(Pot payouts stay link-free on purpose — the daily and weekly pots pay **game credits**, funded
by staked credits. No chain transaction exists for them, and inventing a link would be a lie.)

## UI — the kill feed fills its panel

The feed was capped at a fixed 250px inside a panel that stretches to match the column beside
it, so a full feed was cut off mid-list with dead space underneath. It now grows with its panel
and scrolls inside it. Measured before and after in a headless browser: 250px of list in a 448px
panel (160px of dead space) became 590px of list in a 646px panel (17px, the padding).

# h9-2026-08-20 — ONE CURRENCY, and the board starts quoting prices

## The paper balance is gone

The machine ran two balances: "paper" that refilled 5,000 every day forever, and "credits" from
burning RCX. Both bought the same shots and both earned the same ladder XP, so the daily handout
quietly did the work the token was supposed to do.

Now there is **one currency: CREDITS.** A wallet is granted 5,000 **once**. After that credits
come only from reloads (burned RCX, 1 for 1), pot wins, and the Gearbox. Every shot on the
ladder therefore cost someone something real or something earned.

Nobody loses anything in the change: existing paper balances are folded into credits on first
load, so every player keeps exactly what they were holding.

## The board quotes the level it is asking about

Each question now carries a market line under it, priced off the same oracle the shot settles
on: `NOW $85.40 · CLEARS $85.95` for a pump, `BREAKS $68,691` for a dump, `BAND $2,403 – $2,434`
for the box, both legs for a race. Thresholds are struck from the price at *your* seal, so the
level shown is the level you would get by sealing right now — the card says `STRIKE AT SEAL` for
plain direction questions rather than inventing a number.

A prediction game that makes you guess what "up 0.6%" means in dollars is hiding the instrument.

## The machine's numbers read like an instrument panel

The stat pills under the floor became a proper metric row: **burned forever, shots fired, pot
today, pot season, champion pay, stakers** — one grid, real typography, no decoration. Burned
forever is the on-chain figure, not the simulated one.

Kill-feed retention also went from 24 entries to 40, so the panel carries a real session's
history instead of the last few minutes.

## The right rail stops stretching the page

Every MISS, every seal and every payout was pushing the right column taller, which pushed the
whole page taller — the feed was allowed to grow without limit inside a grid row it was also
defining. It is now a **sticky rail with a fixed height**: it holds the viewport, the list scrolls
inside it, and it ends flush with the panel it lives in. Measured with a full 40-entry feed:
before, the feed rendered 1,312px tall and dragged the page to 1,843px; after, the feed is capped
to the viewport, scrolls internally, and the page is 1,579px — set entirely by the board, not by
how much happened today. Below 920px the rail goes static and the list caps at 56vh.

## Connected-state containment, and a real explainer

Connecting a wallet adds two more panels to the right rail — YOUR CHAMBERS filling with sealed
shots, and RELOAD — and the rail had a fixed height with visible overflow, so on a live account
the panels painted straight out of their column and past the bottom of the board. The rail now
scrolls inside itself, the chamber list and the feed each carry their own bounded scroll
(40vh and 46vh), and the two fixed panels no longer compress. Nothing can leave the column in
any state: empty, connected, or five chambers deep with a full feed.

`? HOW TO PLAY` became **`? HOW IT WORKS — READ THIS FIRST`**, and the panel behind it is now the
whole product in seven sections: the point, how to play, how you win, the economy, the
technology, why it cannot rug you, and what we do not claim — including the on-chain settlement
program's address and the plain statement that it is devnet and the live game still settles on a
server. If someone reads one thing before firing a shot, this is it.

# h11-2026-08-20 — name your own stake, and the podium stops lying

## The podium says why it is empty

After the first rollover the panel still read *"NO PODIUM YET — UNTIL THE FIRST DAILY ROLLOVER,
100% OF EVERY RELOAD BURNS."* The rollover had happened hours earlier. The seat was empty for a
completely different reason: nobody in the day's top three holds RCX, so there is no wallet the
Champion's Cut is allowed to pay.

It now says that, and says what to do about it — hold $RCX, finish top three, and 30% of every
reload lands in your wallet all day. The old wording survives only for the state where it was
actually true: before the first rollover has ever run.

A panel that implies the machine hasn't started when the machine has started is the one kind of
error this project cannot afford.

## Name your own stake

The three tiers — 100 ×1, 500 ×2, 2,500 ×5 — were never arbitrary: they are three points on
**√(stake/100)**. So instead of inventing a rule for custom amounts, the curve was simply made
continuous. Any whole stake between 100 and 2,500 is now allowed, and the field shows its exact
multiplier as you type, computed from the same rule the server scores with — the number on screen
cannot disagree with the number you are awarded.

The design law survives intact, because it lives in the shape of the curve rather than in the
tiers: staking 25× more earns 5× the XP and never 25×, so the ladder cannot be bought — and XP is
still only ever paid on a **HIT**. A bigger stake buys leverage on being right. It buys exactly
nothing on being right.

One disclosed consequence: making the curve continuous moves the 500 preset from a flat ×2 to its
true √5 = **×2.24**. Slightly more XP for that tier than yesterday, and the presets now sit on the
same line as every other amount instead of beside it.

# h12-2026-08-20 — being right pays, and anyone can play

Two structural problems, found by reading our own code rather than by anyone complaining.

## A perfect player still went broke

A HIT awarded XP and nothing else. Credits only ever left a player — the sole way to get any back
was finishing top three at midnight. Add h9's removal of the daily refill and the arithmetic was
brutal: a new wallet got 5,000 credits, roughly ten shots, and then the game ended unless they
bought RCX. **Being right felt like being right and still losing money.**

A hit now returns **1.7× the stake in credits**. Break-even sits near 59% accuracy: beat the market
consistently and you play forever, guess and you run down. You can only ever win credits by risking
credits you already hold, so it is a skill filter rather than a faucet, and no token is ever minted.

The frozen **70 / 30 / 0** split is untouched — every stake is still divided exactly as published.
This is what the game does for winners, not a change to the rule.

The fire panel now states it before you commit: *a hit returns N credits (1.7× your stake) · a miss
feeds the Machine*, and shot history shows the credits each hit returned.

## The people most likely to play were the only ones locked out

`connect()` handed out a demo identity **only to visitors with no wallet installed**. So someone
without Phantom could play freely, while someone *with* Phantom — the person who might actually
connect — hit a disabled button reading CONNECT TO FIRE and could not try the game at all.

Every visitor now gets a **GUEST** identity on arrival and can fire immediately. Guest shots settle
on the same oracle but never enter a ladder, a pot or the feed — enforced on the server, not merely
labelled. The fire button says so: *SEAL THE SHOT — 500 (GUEST, UNRANKED)*.

And once, exactly once, after a guest's first shot settles, the machine asks: *you called that one

# h13-2026-08-20 — you can finally see yourself on the ladder

A leaderboard showing ten strangers is decoration. What makes a ladder pull is knowing where *you*

The machine now says all four things, on the PLAY tab and again above today's ladder, updating

# h14-2026-08-20 — THE FLEET: the Machine gets agents

A prediction arcade with three players has nothing to watch and nobody to beat. Four agents fix
that without faking a single thing.

**MOMENTUM** rides the last hour of drift. **REVERSION** is its strict inverse — it fades the move
and assumes it overshot. **VOLATILITY** plays for movement and never for direction; it takes the
outside of every band. **CONTRARIAN** fights the Warden, taking the other side of the house AI
every hour, on principle.

Each publishes its method, fires one real call an hour on the same board every player sees, settles
on the same Pyth price as everyone else, and accrues a public record **including its losses** —
exactly the standard the Warden has been held to since day one. The calls are written into the
hash-chained log at seal time, so an agent cannot quietly change its mind before the window closes.

The first hour already produced what it was built for: MOMENTUM and REVERSION took opposite sides
of the same question, head to head, in public, with a countdown running.

**The hard rule, printed on the panel:** agents never enter a ladder, a pot, the podium or the burn
counter. They stake nothing and they win nothing. Every counter on the site still describes humans
only. They exist to be beaten.

*(One design note worth recording: REVERSION was originally implemented by negating MOMENTUM's
drift input, which made the two agents identical whenever drift was flat — including the very first
hour, when there is no prior price at all. It now strictly inverts MOMENTUM's output instead.)*
