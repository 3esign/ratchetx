# RATCHET — hardening audit, 20 August 2026

Three independent adversarial reads of `api/game.js`, `lib/*.js`, `index.html` and
`onchain/ratchet_seal_lib_v1.rs`. Every finding below was re-verified against the source
before it was written down.

## Verdict

**It does not hold yet.** The oracle work is solid — that part is genuinely hardened. The
game economy around it is not. There is one defect that, on its own, lets a patient player
win close to 100% of their shots, and it is live right now.

The good news is the arithmetic of when you found it: 30 shots, 6 wallets on the season
ladder, an empty podium, and no champion payment has ever occurred. Nothing has been stolen,
because there is nothing yet worth stealing. That window closes the moment the outreach
lands.

---

## The six that matter

Ranked by "converts to real money or breaks a claim published on the site."

### 1 · CRITICAL — every expired shot is a free option

`api/game.js:585-592`, `api/game.js:696-704`

`settle()` uses `prices[s.feed]` — the price at the moment of settling, **not** the price at
expiry. There is no historical lookup anywhere in the codebase, and a Pyth push account only
holds the latest update, so there is nothing to look one up from.

`settle()` has exactly two call sites and both are scoped to one named wallet. No cron, no
sweeper, nothing else in the system will ever settle your shot. So:

```
seal a shot → close the tab → the shot expires and just sits there
              → watch the market → when it is in your favour:
GET /api/game?action=state&wallet=<your wallet>
```

That GET requires **no signature**. `verifyAuth` runs on `shot`, `duel`, `stake`, `reload`
and `anchor` — not on `state`.

A sealed shot past its window is therefore not a resolved bet. It is an American option with
a free, unbounded exercise right, held by the only person who knows it exists. Price is
recurrent, so P(eventually favourable) → 1. Modelled against SOL's own stated 0.75%/hr
volatility, a shot currently losing by 0.5% has a **median wait of 68 minutes** and a 95%
chance of coming good within a week.

`HIT_PAYOUT = 1.7` was chosen for a 59% break-even. This drives accuracy toward 100%.

**Same root, two more attacks.** Because `state?wallet=` is unauthenticated *and* writes,
and because `/api/snapshot` publishes every player's open shots by full wallet, you can also
force-settle a **rival** at the worst moment for them — resetting their streak and denying
them the ladder XP that pays the daily pot.

### 2 · HIGH — the sealed side leaks through `xp`

`api/game.js:824-829`, stripped at `api/game.js:710` and `api/snapshot.js:54`

The strip removes `side` and `salt`. It does not remove `xp` — and `xp` is computed as
`baseXp × stakeMult(stake) × xpMult`, where `xpMult` is `yesMult` or `noMult` depending on
the side. `baseXp` and `noMult` are deterministic from the public hourly board, and `stake`
and `label` also survive the strip. So:

```
xpMult = xp / (baseXp × √(stake/100))
```

At every legal stake, for THE PUMP, THE DUMP, THE BOX and every DUEL, the two sides produce
values that never collide. A DUEL at 2,500 credits is `56` for *with* and `238` for
*against*.

This contradicts `README.md`: *"no one (including us) can read an open shot's side…
Spectator APIs and snapshots never carry open sides."* The commitment scheme itself is
sound — 64-bit CSPRNG salt, correct reveal — but the side walks out through a side channel
next to it. It also aims the forced-settlement attack above.

`dir` and `race` are safe: neither defines a multiplier, so `xpMult` is always 1.

The harness misses this because it only tests `SOL5`, a `dir` shot, and only asserts
`!('side' in openShot)`.

### 3 · HIGH — guest stakes feed the real pot

`api/game.js:650-662`

`takeStake` is the only function on the seal path with no `isDemo` guard. Guest IDs are
generated in the browser (`'demo-'+Math.random().toString(36).slice(2,6)`), skip `verifyAuth`
entirely, and each one is minted 5,000 credits on first sight.

Every farmed guest identity contributes to `st.burned`, `st.potD`, `st.pot`, `st.shots` and
ratchets `st.floor` — permanently, since the floor is monotone.

`CHANGES_2026-08-19.md` states: *"Guest shots settle on the same oracle but never enter a
ladder, a pot or the feed — enforced on the server, not merely labelled."* The ladder and
feed guards are real and correct. **The pot guard does not exist.** And the pot is paid out
in credits to real wallets at 00:00 UTC.

It also flows into the daily balance root that gets anchored on Solana — the artifact
described in-code as airdrop fairness.

### 4 · HIGH — a free keypair is a ranked player

`api/game.js:203`, `lib/verify.js:34-52`

`verifyAuth` proves possession of an Ed25519 private key. Generating one is free and offline.
Every new pubkey is a new record with 5,000 credits, and because it isn't prefixed `demo-`
it ranks, seats the podium, and collects the Champion's Cut in **real RCX**.

The h11 fix closed exactly half of this — it filtered `demo-*` after noticing that *"a script
could farm the paying ladder with free throwaway identities."* A locally generated keypair is
an equally free throwaway identity.

The HOLDER RULE doesn't stop it either: a Sybil that never sells always satisfies
`bal >= earned7 × 0.5`.

### 5 · HIGH — ladder XP is double-countable, and a credited reload can be erased

`api/game.js:224-231` — `bumpLadder` is a read-modify-write on a key **separate** from the
player blob. Player fields self-heal under last-write-wins because they all derive from one
snapshot; a relative increment on another key does not. Concurrent settles of the same shot
each add again. The per-instance rate limiter is structurally blind to this, because a
concurrent burst fans out across instances.

`api/game.js:890-895` is worse in kind if rarer: a `reload` credits the player and saves,
while a concurrent `state` request that loaded the record *before* the credit writes its own
stale snapshot back afterwards. The credit vanishes — but `sig:<sig>` is already consumed, so
it can never be reclaimed. **Real RCX destroyed, no credit, no retry, no recovery path.**

### 6 · MEDIUM — the quiet-market payout is asymmetric

`api/game.js:608-610`

`dir` and `race` VOID on a tie and refund. `thr`, `thrDown` and `range` have no EPS band at
all — if the price doesn't move, **NO wins and is paid the full 1.7×**. NO is the
discounted-XP side on all three. So a non-event pays one side in full.

The Fleet also settles THE BOX by a different rule than players do (`px > hi || px < lo`
versus `>= pct`) — agents and humans scored on the same question by two different rules.

## The fix, and why it is smaller than it looks

Most of the severity above collapses to **one** root cause: the exit price is whatever
happens to be current when someone triggers a settle, and anyone can trigger one.

**Sample the oracle into an hourly bucket, and settle from the bucket.**

```
writer   (rate-limited to once per 60s, the sponsored-feed heartbeat):
         append {t, prices} to px:<YYYY-MM-DD-HH>
settler: bucket = px:<hour containing s.exp>
         exit   = first sample with t >= s.exp
         if no such sample exists -> VOID and refund
```

This is the same rule the on-chain program already enforces —
`prev_publish_time < expiry <= publish_time`, the first price at or after the window closed —
expressed in the only form available off-chain.

What it buys:

- **Finding 1 dies outright.** The exit price no longer depends on when a settle is
  triggered, so holding a shot gains you nothing and the option evaporates.
- **The rival attack dies with it** — a stranger settling your shot now produces the same
  number you would have produced.
- **`state?wallet=` no longer needs authenticating**, which keeps the liveness mechanism
  intact. *Who* cranks stops mattering, which was the whole point of the on-chain rule.
- **The exit price becomes publishable.** Anyone can read the bucket and recompute the
  settlement. That is a proof-page win, not just a patch.

Cost: ~1,440 KV writes/day, one small read per settle. Buckets expire after 48h except a
coarse tail for the 24-hour chamber.

### Order of work

**Stop the bleeding — before any outreach drives traffic**

1. Price buckets + deterministic settle (kills 1, and the rival attack)
2. Strip `xp` from spectator open shots; return it only in the owner's fire response (kills 2)
3. `isDemo` guard in `takeStake` — deduct the guest's credits, skip the pot/burn accounting (kills 3)
4. EPS band on `thr`/`thrDown`/`range`; align the Fleet's BOX rule with the player's (kills 6)

**Hardening — before the podium is worth taking**

5. Ladders to a Redis sorted set — `ZINCRBY` is atomic and makes top-N reads cheaper (kills half of 5)
6. Credits out of the JSON blob into an atomic counter (kills the reload-erasure half of 5)
7. Sybil resistance on the welcome grant (4). The cleanest version costs the attacker
   something the honest player already spent: **grant credits on first RCX touch rather than
   on first sight of a pubkey.** Guests still play unranked and free; ranking requires having
   held the token once.

**Only then — agents**

## On opening this to agents

It is the right next build and it is currently blocked by finding 1.

An agent API is a machine that polls endpoints on a schedule and acts on returns. Today that
machine would discover within hours that not-settling is worth more than settling, and the
first competent agent would farm the ladder to exhaustion. You would be handing the exploit
an execution engine.

Once settlement is deterministic, the agent product is strong, and you already have most of it:

- **The Fleet is the reference implementation.** Four agents already call the same board, on
  the same oracle, under the same sealing rules, with public records. The API is that,
  exposed.
- **Register an agent** — POST an endpoint URL and a name. Each hour RATCHET calls it with
  the board and gets back sealed calls. The agent's key signs; the record is public and
  permanent, wins and losses.
- **Duel any agent** at odds derived from its *real* accuracy, not a house number.
- **The scoreboard is the product.** A public, oracle-settled, tamper-evident accuracy
  ranking of trading agents is a thing that does not really exist yet, and it is worth more
  than the game around it. Anyone building a trading bot wants a neutral place to prove it
  works.

That last point is why this ordering matters: an agent leaderboard is only interesting if the
scores are real. Ship it on top of a settlement rule anyone can exploit and it is worth
nothing. Ship it on top of a deterministic one and it is the most defensible thing in the
project.

## What is already correct — do not "fix" these

Worth stating plainly, because a list this long can read as though nothing works.

- **The oracle path.** Owner check, discriminator, `Full` verification level, feed-id binding,
  staleness bound, honest degradation. This week's work holds up.
- **Auth identity.** The wallet is bound inside the signed message and verified against the
  pubkey decoded from that same address. **You cannot act as another wallet without their key.**
  No confused deputy.
- **`isDemo` ∩ `isWalletShaped` = ∅** — `-` is not in the base58 alphabet, so a guest identity
  can never collide with a real wallet.
- **Demo filtering on ladders, feed, pot ranking and podium** — all real and correct. Only
  the global counters leak.
- **Commit-reveal itself** — 64-bit CSPRNG salt, correct hash, verifiable reveal.
- **`setnxJSON`** genuinely closes the pot-payout, burn-replay and anchor-replay races.
- **`p._existed || changed`** stops `state?wallet=<junk>` minting records.
- **Payout rounding** — every `Math.floor` rounds toward the house.
- **`durable`** cannot report true while on the in-memory fallback; `setJSON` rethrows rather
  than silently degrading.
- **Reload validation** — non-podium outflow is refused and the 30% cut is capped.
- **Thresholds struck from the seal-time snapshot** — board rotation cannot retro-move them.

## One correction to the repo

`onchain/ratchet_seal_lib_v1.rs:41` declares `VOID_AFTER_SECS` and never uses it. There is no
`void` instruction in the program — only `seal`, `settle`, `reveal`, `close_shot`. The
comments describing a void path describe behaviour that does not exist, and because
`CloseShot` requires state `Revealed`, an unsettleable shot has its rent locked permanently.
Either implement `void` or delete the constant and the comments.

`README.md` also still describes the v0 `[expiry, expiry+60]` window and cites the 288-line
`ratchet_seal_lib.rs`; the live file is the 347-line `_v1` with the strict crossing rule.
