- **minOpenLeadSeconds**: 60. **The floor is derived; the value is taste.** It must be *strictly* greater than `maxFutureSkewSeconds` (30), because a Need for target `T` opens as early as clock `C = T - lead`, the program accepts a print up to `clock + skew` ahead, and such a print is already admissible for `T` (`publish_time >= T`) iff `skew >= lead`. Below that line the settling price can be read off the sponsored account **before the shot is committed**, which is the one thing forward-only entry exists to prevent. Enforced by `lib.rs::validate_spec`; proved in `lifecycle.rs::lead_above_skew_is_exactly_the_condition_for_an_unknowable_price`. Nothing else in the program closes it — the only slot bound on a candidate is `posted_slot > registered_slot`, which ties the print to the *spec's registration* and never to the *Need's opening*.

  The previous value was **30, equal to the skew**, which is exactly the boundary case and could not register. Set to one full grid so a player commits at least a whole round ahead, leaving 29 s of margin over the floor. **The bare minimum that registers is 31.** Both are one line and neither needs a proof: how far ahead a shot must be placed is a question about what the game feels like, and it is the owner's.

- **maxPreTargetGapSeconds**: 0, on every feed. NOT a choice. Under adapter 2 (MIN-CAPTURE) `prev_publish_time` is not in the predicate at all, so a non-zero value would be a dead number living inside `canonical_policy_bytes` and therefore inside every spec hash, forever. `lib.rs::validate_spec` refuses adapter 2 with any other value. The previous 1 was correct under adapter 1 and became invalid the moment the adapter changed to 2 — two individually correct edits that were jointly wrong.
- **maxPostTargetLagSeconds**: 59 on every feed, and it is **DERIVED, not measured**. `targetGridSeconds - 1`. Admissibility for target `T` is `[T, T+lag]`, so coverage is monotonically non-decreasing in `lag`; and `lag < grid` is necessary and sufficient for a print to belong to at most one target, because a print serves both `T` and `T+grid` iff `lag >= grid`. Therefore `grid - 1` is the coverage optimum **for every feed, without knowing anything about that feed's cadence**. Enforced at registration by `lib.rs::validate_spec`; proved exhaustively over every lag for grids 30, 60 and 300 in `lifecycle.rs::lag_below_grid_is_exactly_the_condition_for_unique_target_assignment`. No sample can establish it and no sample can refute it.

  *Superseded values, kept because one of them was a real preference and not an error:* 120 for ETH/BONK/PUMP/JUP/WIF was **twice the grid** and would have let one print settle two consecutive targets — a shot on 12:00:00 and a shot on 12:01:00 resolving at the same price, permanently, for this economy. That one was a defect. But **30 for SOL/BTC was a legitimate quality-versus-liveness preference**: a shorter window puts the settling print closer to the target, at the cost of voiding when the only available print is far. Raising it to 59 trades price quality for liveness on the two fastest feeds, where measured p99 first-print lag is 4 s. A *smaller* lag stays legitimate; a larger one is not a preference, it is a broken game. If the owner wants that quality back, 30 is one line and needs no proof.

  *The measurement is not gone, it is just no longer load-bearing:* `docs/reviews/cadence/cadence-2026-09-05.summary.json`, 442 of 442 targets, p99 4 s (SOL/BTC) and 51–52 s (the other five). It answers "how alive is it", which is a liveness question that the chain will answer better after launch — a target with no admissible print voids and refunds in full, and the void rate is public on-chain state.
