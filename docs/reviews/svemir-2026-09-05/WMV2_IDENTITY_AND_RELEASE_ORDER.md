Dependency direction matters. Core G2 publishes its own WorkManifest and permanent completion WorkPages. `complete_optional_work` returns successfully when its canonical optional page/reservation is absent; it neither requires nor invokes a Work Market address. WMv2 consumes the producer's public manifest/accounts. In WMv2, `FundRcxVoucher` lines 419–424 require the exact producer ProgramData and `upgrade_authority_address.is_none()`. Voucher seeds at lines 431–438 include the producer program, subject, work kind, sponsor and nonce. The work-manifest PDA derives under the producer address at lines 688–696.

My decision: this finding must not reopen or block the isolated ANV Core/C8 Timepin build. It blocks releasing WMv2 under the fixture identity. Preserve the complete game and its work-record ABI. Select a dedicated verified WMv2 deployment identity in the build-owner lane, then retarget its source/fixtures, build and re-prove its exact artifact separately. Do not assume the held v1 Edwr key is a v2 key. There is no reason to edit Core logic merely to replace WMv2's own address.

Release order from the code: prove Core/Timepin lifecycle with callers funding permissionless operations; obtain the separate authorization for producer freeze; then prove actual RCX vouchers against those immutable producers while WMv2 remains upgradeable; only afterward consider WMv2's own freeze. Trying to require real producer vouchers before the producer freeze creates a circular gate. Work Market is optional for Core transitions but relevant to paying third-party workers; optional does not mean removed from the finished project.

Honest verdict: source identity and dependency/authority constraints reviewed, no WMv2 SBF or live execution, no new key generation and no deployment/freeze/signing. Permissionless execution still needs a caller and transaction funding; this review makes no automatic-liveness or zero-cost claim.
